# Lecture 1: The Foundations of Style

**Lessons from Strunk & White's *The Elements of Style***

*Adapted for Economics and Social Science Writing*

李学恒，中山大学岭南学院，2025年11月18日

---

## Good Writing is Good Thinking

> **Writing is not a final, decorative step.**
> **Writing IS the process of discovering and clarifying your ideas.**

Your abstract reveals whether you understand your own contribution.
Your introduction shows whether you grasp why your work matters.

---

## The Challenge

Most academic writing suffers from:

- **Wordiness** - 200 words where 50 would do
- **Vagueness** - "significant effects" instead of precise magnitudes
- **Passivity** - "It is shown that" instead of "We show"
- **Weak endings** - Paragraphs that trail off instead of landing

We can do better.

---

## Today's Goal

Move beyond mere correctness to prose that is:

- **Clear** - Impossible to misunderstand
- **Precise** - Specific, not vague
- **Economical** - Every word earns its place
- **Persuasive** - Structured for maximum impact

---

## Our Guide

**William Strunk Jr.'s** *The Elements of Style* (1918)

A complete system for clear, vigorous writing.

> **43 rules organized into three parts:**
> **I. Elementary Rules of Usage (1-11)**
> **II. Elementary Principles of Composition (12-22)**
> **V. An Approach to Style (21 reminders)**

---



## Today's Focus

**From the 43 rules, we'll master the 7 most critical for academic writing:**

> **Foundation:** Rules 17, 16, 15 (omit needless words, be specific, use positive form)
> **Structure:** Rules 14, 20, 19 (active voice, word order, parallelism)
> **Impact:** Rule 22 (emphatic endings)

These seven rules will transform your writing from correct to compelling.

**Note:** The other 36 rules are equally important but more mechanical. Today we focus on principles that change how you think about writing.

# Rule 17: Omit Needless Words

**The Golden Rule of All Writing**

---

## The Principle

> Vigorous writing is concise. A sentence should contain no unnecessary words, a paragraph no unnecessary sentences, for the same reason that a drawing should have no unnecessary lines and a machine no unnecessary parts.

—William Strunk Jr.

**This does not mean make all sentences short.**
**It means make every word tell.**

---

## Sentence-Level Examples

**Economics/Social Science Writing:**

❌ The question as to whether inflation is rising
✓ Whether inflation is rising

❌ There is no doubt that the policy failed
✓ The policy clearly failed

❌ The study is aimed at investigating the effects of
✓ The study investigates how

❌ Our findings provide evidence in support of the hypothesis
✓ Our findings support the hypothesis

---

## More Sentence Examples

❌ Due to the fact that markets are volatile
✓ Because markets are volatile

❌ At this point in time, we observe
✓ Now we observe

❌ In the event that prices fall
✓ If prices fall

❌ For the purpose of testing the model
✓ To test the model

---

## Academic Writing: Before & After

❌ **Wordy Abstract**
> This paper aims to explore the impact that remote work policies have had on employee productivity and satisfaction within the technology sector. In light of the unprecedented global circumstances that have transpired over the past few years, it has become increasingly evident that many organizations have been compelled to rapidly adapt their operational frameworks. The findings from our investigation suggest that there exists a significant relationship between flexible work arrangements and various measures of employee well-being, which has important implications for human resource management practices.

---

## Academic Writing: Before & After

✓ **Concise Abstract (25 words)**
> Remote work increases productivity by 12% and satisfaction by 18% for tech employees. Survey data from 4,200 workers at 150 technology firms (2021-2023).

**Savings: 72 words (74% reduction)**

---

## What Was Cut?

- ~~"This paper aims to explore the impact that"~~ → Just state what you found
- ~~"have had on"~~ → "affects" (one verb)
- ~~"In light of the unprecedented global circumstances that have transpired"~~ → COVID happened, not your contribution
- ~~"it has become increasingly evident that"~~ → Delete (empty phrase)
- ~~"compelled to rapidly adapt their operational frameworks"~~ → "changed how they work"
- ~~"The findings from our investigation suggest that there exists a significant relationship"~~ → "We find" (just say what you found)
- ~~"various measures of employee well-being"~~ → Name them or don't mention them
- ~~"which has important implications for"~~ → "This suggests" or just end the sentence

---

## Common Culprits: Delete These Immediately

| **Wordy Expression** | **Concise Alternative** |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| the question as to whether | whether |
| there is no doubt that | doubtless / clearly |
| in spite of the fact that | although / though |
| due to the fact that | because / since |
| the reason why is that | because |
| at this point in time | now |
| in the event that | if |
| for the purpose of | to / for |

---

## Common Culprits: Delete These Immediately

| **Wordy Expression** | **Concise Alternative** |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| it is important to note that | [delete] |
| it should be emphasized that | [delete] |
| it is worth noting that | [delete] |
| the purpose of this study is to | [delete - just state it] |
| this is a subject that | this subject |
| he is a man who | he |

## Economics Example

❌ **Wordy (From real economics student paper)**
> The empirical findings from our econometric analysis indicate that there exists a statistically significant correlation between central bank policy rate changes and subsequent movements in inflation expectations. Furthermore, the evidence suggests that this relationship appears to be mediated primarily through what is commonly referred to as the forward guidance mechanism, which has important implications for the conduct of monetary policy.

---

## Economics Example

✓ **Concise (25 words)**
> Monetary policy affects inflation expectations through forward guidance. A 1 percentage point rate increase reduces expectations by 0.3 percentage points within two quarters.

**What changed:** Deleted filler, added precision, stated the finding directly.

---

## Introduction Example

❌ **Wordy (From finance journal submission)**
> Given the increasing complexity of modern corporate finance, there has been substantial scholarly attention devoted to understanding the various behavioral factors that may influence executive decision-making processes. Specifically, the phenomenon of CEO overconfidence has emerged as a particularly salient area of investigation within the broader corporate governance literature. This research represents an attempt to extend our understanding by empirically examining whether significant differences exist in the investment patterns exhibited by overconfident chief executive officers relative to their more moderate counterparts.

---

## Introduction Example

✓ **Concise (35 words)**
> Overconfident CEOs invest 12% more than their peers in our analysis of 1,200 firms over 15 years (capex/assets: 8.4% vs. 7.5%). This overinvestment doubles in firms with weak board monitoring, suggesting governance mitigates behavioral biases.

**Savings: 44 words (56% reduction)**
**Bonus: Now includes your finding!**

---

## The "Fact That" Disease

**Never write "the fact that"** - it is always unnecessary.

❌ owing to the fact that → **since / because**
❌ in spite of the fact that → **though / although**
❌ call your attention to the fact that → **remind you / note**
❌ I was unaware of the fact that → **I did not know**
❌ the fact that he had failed → **his failure**
❌ the fact that I had arrived → **my arrival**

**Test:** Every instance can be deleted or replaced with one word.

---

## Connection to the Academic Writing Blacklist

**Rule 17 directly fights these offenders:**

- ~~robust~~ - "The results are robust" = "The results hold"
- ~~various~~ - "Various factors" = Name them or delete
- ~~substantial~~ - "Substantial effect" = Give the magnitude
- ~~comprehensive~~ - "Comprehensive analysis" = What kind of analysis?
- ~~important to note~~ - Always delete
- ~~interesting~~ - "Interesting pattern" = Describe the pattern

**Principle:** If a word doesn't strengthen the claim, cut it.

---

## Exercise: Cut the Deadwood

Reduce this to under 25 words:

> It should be noted that the results of our econometric estimation suggest there exists a statistically significant positive association between educational attainment and income levels, which aligns with predictions derived from human capital theory.

**Your turn:** Count to 10, then we'll see the answer.

---

## Exercise: Answer

> ~~It should be noted that the results of~~ our ~~econometric estimation~~ regression ~~suggest there exists a statistically significant positive association between educational attainment and income levels~~ **shows education increases earnings**, ~~which aligns with predictions derived from~~ consistent with human capital theory.

**Better (10 words):**
> Our regression shows education increases earnings, consistent with human capital theory.

**Even Better (12 words):**
> Each year of education increases earnings by 8% (p < 0.01), supporting human capital theory.

# Rule 16: Use Definite, Specific, Concrete Language

**Prefer the specific to the general, the definite to the vague, the concrete to the abstract.**

---

## The Principle

Strunk & White's example:

❌ **Vague:** A period of unfavorable weather set in.
✓ **Concrete:** It rained every day for a week.

**Academic translation:**

❌ **Vague:** The intervention had a significant positive effect.
✓ **Concrete:** The intervention increased test scores by 0.4 standard deviations.

---

## Sentence-Level Examples

**From Economics/Social Science Writing:**

❌ The effect is substantial
✓ The effect is 12 percentage points (from 23% to 35%)

❌ We use a large sample
✓ We use a sample of 4,237 firms

❌ Several factors influence outcomes
✓ Income, education, and age influence outcomes

❌ The relationship is statistically significant
✓ The correlation is r = 0.45 (p < 0.001)

---

## More Sentence Examples

❌ Overconfident CEOs make different decisions
✓ Overconfident CEOs invest 12% more (8.4% vs. 7.5% of assets)

❌ The intervention was successful
✓ The intervention reduced dropout rates from 18% to 9%

❌ Performance improved considerably
✓ Performance improved by 23% (from 65 to 80 points)

❌ The policy had notable effects
✓ The policy increased employment by 2,400 jobs

---

## Vague vs. Precise: Abstract Comparison

❌ **Vague (From actual student paper on education)**
> Our findings provide strong evidence that the tutoring intervention leads to measurable improvements in student outcomes. Several background variables demonstrate notable relationships with treatment effectiveness. The estimated effects remain stable under alternative estimation approaches and carry meaningful implications for educational policy design.

**What's wrong?** No numbers, no specifics, no actual findings.

---

## Vague vs. Precise: Abstract Comparison

✓ **Precise (Data-Driven Abstract)**
> One-on-one tutoring increases reading scores by 0.6 grade levels (n = 487). Students below grade level improve twice as much as those at grade level (1.2 vs. 0.6 grade levels). Effects persist through the following school year, suggesting targeted tutoring can close achievement gaps efficiently.

**What changed:** Every claim now has specific numbers (effect sizes, sample size, comparisons).

---

## The "Various/Several/Multiple" Problem

These words are almost always evasions:

❌ **Various factors affect the outcome**
✓ **Income, education, and age affect the outcome**

❌ **We tested several specifications**
✓ **We tested OLS, IV, and difference-in-differences**

❌ **Multiple channels could explain this**
✓ **Selection bias or peer effects could explain this**

**Rule:** If you can name them, name them. If you can't, you don't understand them.

---

## Economics Example: Magnitudes Matter

❌ **Vague**
> Our model shows that monetary policy has a significant impact on inflation expectations through various channels.

✓ **Specific**
> A 1 percentage point increase in the policy rate reduces inflation expectations by 0.3 percentage points within two quarters (β = -0.31, SE = 0.08), operating through the forward guidance channel.

**What changed:** Exact magnitudes, precise timing, specific mechanism, reported uncertainty.

---

## Results Section Example

❌ **Vague**
> Table 2 shows the main results. The coefficient on our treatment variable is positive and statistically significant. This finding is robust to including control variables.

✓ **Specific**
> Table 2 shows treated firms invest 12 percentage points more than control firms (investment/assets: 23% vs. 11%, β = 0.12, p = 0.003). Adding industry and year fixed effects strengthens the result (β = 0.15, p < 0.001).

**Bonus:** Readers can evaluate your claim without flipping to the table.

---

## The "Substantial/Considerable/Significant" Problem

**Never use these adjectives for magnitudes:**

❌ substantial increase → **X% increase / X unit increase**
❌ considerable evidence → **n studies find... / 8 of 10 studies show...**
❌ significant correlation → **r = 0.45, p < 0.001**
❌ marked improvement → **improved from X to Y (Z% increase)**
❌ notable difference → **Group A: 65%, Group B: 43%, p = 0.02**

**Rule:** If you measured it, report the measurement.

---

## Exercise: Add Precision

Make this specific (you can invent plausible numbers):

> The results of our organizational study reveal that a considerable percentage of managerial respondents utilize analytical tools in their decision-making processes. There appears to be a systematic relationship between managerial age cohort and technology adoption patterns, with this difference achieving conventional levels of statistical significance.

**Your turn:** What numbers would make this concrete?

---

## Exercise: Answer

> We surveyed ~~a large sample of~~ **347** managers and found that ~~a significant proportion~~ **68%** reported using data analytics in decision-making. ~~Younger~~ **Managers under 40** were ~~more likely~~ **twice as likely** to adopt these tools ~~, and the difference was statistically significant~~ **(73% vs. 35%, p < 0.001)**.

**Result:** Reader now knows the sample size, adoption rate, age cutoff, and effect size.

# Rule 15: Put Statements in Positive Form

**Make definite assertions. Avoid tame, colorless, hesitating, noncommittal language.**

---

## The Principle

Strunk & White's example:

❌ **Negative:** He was not very often on time.
✓ **Positive:** He usually came late.

**Academic translation:**

❌ **Negative/Hedged:** The results do not provide evidence against the hypothesis.
✓ **Positive:** The results support the hypothesis.

---

## Sentence-Level Examples

**From Economics/Social Science Writing:**

❌ The coefficient is not insignificant
✓ The coefficient is significant (β = 0.18, p = 0.02)

❌ We do not find that education is unimportant
✓ Education significantly increases earnings (β = 0.08, p < 0.01)

❌ The policy was not unsuccessful
✓ The policy succeeded (employment increased 15%)

❌ This finding is not inconsistent with theory
✓ This finding confirms/supports the theory

---

## More Sentence Examples

❌ Overconfidence may not be unrelated to investment
✓ Overconfidence increases investment by 12%

❌ The results do not contradict our hypothesis
✓ The results support our hypothesis

❌ We cannot rule out the possibility that
✓ Our evidence suggests that / We find that

❌ It is not implausible that Z mediates the effect
✓ Z likely mediates the effect / Z mediates the effect

---

## State What IS, Not What IS NOT

❌ **Negative**
> The data do not show that education is unimportant for earnings. Our estimates are not inconsistent with human capital theory.

✓ **Positive**
> The data show education significantly increases earnings. Our estimates support human capital theory.

**Why?** The reader wants to know what you found, not what you failed to find.

---

## The Hedging Problem

Academic writers over-rely on uncertainty markers:

❌ **Excessive Hedging**
> Our results may suggest that CEO overconfidence could potentially have an impact on investment decisions, which might be interpreted as evidence that behavioral factors may play a role.

**Word count of hedges:** may (2), could (1), potentially (1), might (1) = **5 hedges in one sentence!**

---

## The Hedging Problem

✓ **Appropriate Certainty**
> CEO overconfidence increases investment by 12% (p = 0.003). This suggests behavioral factors influence corporate decisions.

**When to hedge:**
- When genuinely uncertain: "This pattern may reflect selection bias"
- When interpreting: "These findings suggest (not prove) that..."
- When speculating: "Future research could test whether..."

**When NOT to hedge:** When reporting your actual results!

---

## Hypothesis Statements

❌ **Weak (Negative/Hedged)**
> We do not expect that firms without female board members will be less likely to adopt family-friendly policies.

**Translation:** I'm stating a hypothesis in negative form with double negatives.

✓ **Strong (Positive)**
> **H1:** Firms with female board members are more likely to adopt family-friendly policies.

**Better:** Clear, testable, states what you expect to find.

---

## Discussion Section Example

❌ **Negative/Weak**
> Our findings do not contradict prior research. We did not find evidence that the alternative explanation accounts for our results. This does not mean that behavioral factors are unimportant.

✓ **Positive/Strong**
> Our findings confirm prior research. Our tests rule out the alternative explanation. Behavioral factors matter: overconfident CEOs invest 12% more, controlling for firm characteristics.

**Every sentence now makes a claim, not a non-claim.**

---

## When Negative IS Appropriate

**Use negative form when reporting null results:**

✓ We find **no evidence** that the placebo treatment affects outcomes (β = 0.02, p = 0.73).

✓ The falsification test **does not reject** the null hypothesis (p = 0.45), suggesting our identifying assumption holds.

✓ Industry fixed effects **do not change** the coefficient (β = 0.12 in both specifications).

**These are positive claims about negative findings.**

---

## Exercise: Make It Positive

Rewrite in positive form:

> The control variables do not appear to be statistically significant. This is not surprising given that prior studies have not found strong evidence for these relationships. Our findings are not inconsistent with this literature.

**Your turn:** What are you actually saying?

---

## Exercise: Answer

Original version says nothing clearly. Better version:

> The control variables are statistically insignificant (all p > 0.10), consistent with prior studies (Smith 2019; Jones 2020). Our findings confirm this pattern: income and education do not predict treatment take-up in our setting.

**What changed:**
- ~~do not appear to be~~ → **are** (state the fact)
- ~~not surprising~~ → **consistent** (positive framing)
- ~~not inconsistent~~ → **confirm** (say what you mean!)
- Added specifics (p-values, citations, which variables)

# Rule 14: Use the Active Voice

**The active voice is usually more direct and vigorous than the passive.**

---

## The Principle

Strunk & White's example:

❌ **Passive:** My first visit to Boston will always be remembered by me.
✓ **Active:** I shall always remember my first visit to Boston.

**Academic translation:**

❌ **Passive:** The relationship between X and Y was examined.
✓ **Active:** We examine how X affects Y.

---

## Sentence-Level Examples

**From Economics/Social Science Writing:**

❌ It was found that overconfidence increases investment
✓ We find overconfidence increases investment

❌ The data were analyzed using regression
✓ We analyze the data using regression

❌ It is shown in Table 2 that the effect is positive
✓ Table 2 shows the effect is positive

❌ It is argued that behavioral factors matter
✓ We argue behavioral factors matter

---

## More Sentence Examples

❌ The hypothesis was tested using experimental data
✓ We test the hypothesis using experimental data

❌ It has been suggested by prior research that
✓ Prior research suggests / Smith (2019) suggests

❌ The results were found to be robust
✓ The results are robust / We find robust results

❌ Three mechanisms were identified
✓ We identify three mechanisms

---

## The Passive Plague in Abstracts

❌ **Passive Abstract (From actual management journal submission)**
> The impact of leadership style on employee engagement is investigated in this research. It was found that transformational leadership behaviors are positively correlated with employee motivation levels. Survey responses from 3,500 employees across 120 organizations were collected and analyzed using structural equation modeling. Multiple mediation pathways through which leadership affects engagement were identified.

**What's wrong?** No clear agent. Who did this work? Who found what?

---

## The Passive Plague in Abstracts

✓ **Active Abstract (Strong)**
> We examine how leadership style affects employee engagement. Using survey data from 3,500 employees across 120 organizations, we find transformational leadership increases engagement scores by 23%. This effect operates primarily through improved communication and perceived organizational support.

**What changed:**
- ~~is investigated~~ → **We examine**
- ~~It was found~~ → **we find**
- ~~were collected and analyzed~~ → **Using survey data** (data = instrument, not agent)
- ~~were identified~~ → **This effect operates through** (mechanism is the agent)
- Clear actors throughout

---

## Common Passive Offenders

Delete these constructions:

- ~~It is shown that~~ → **We show / Our results show**
- ~~It was found that~~ → **We find / The data show**
- ~~It is argued that~~ → **We argue / Smith (2019) argues**
- ~~It has been suggested~~ → **Prior work suggests / Jones (2020) suggests**
- ~~It can be seen that~~ → **Table 2 shows / The figure reveals**

**Pattern:** Replace "It is X that" with an actual subject doing X.

---

## When Passive IS Appropriate

**Use passive voice when the agent is irrelevant or obvious:**

✓ The model **is estimated** using GMM.
✓ The data **were collected** from firm 10-K filings.
✓ The null hypothesis **is not rejected** (J-statistic = 12.4, p = 0.19).
✓ Robust standard errors **are reported** in parentheses.

**Why passive works here:** The method/test is the focus, not who performed it. Everyone knows *you* did it.

---

## Methods Section: Mixed Voice

A well-written methods section uses both:

> We estimate the model using GMM (active - you're explaining your choice). The instruments **are** industry averages and lagged values (passive - describing the set). We test overidentification with the J-statistic (active - your test). The null hypothesis **is not rejected** (passive - conventional phrasing), suggesting valid instruments. We also estimate OLS and IV specifications (active - your robustness checks).

**Principle:** Active for your choices/actions, passive for technical descriptions.

---

## Exercise: Activate These Sentences

Rewrite in active voice:

> It is shown in Table 3 that the treatment effect is positive and significant. This finding was not expected based on prior literature. The robustness of the result was verified through several alternative specifications.

**Your turn:** Who is doing what?

---

## Exercise: Answer

> Table 3 shows the treatment effect is positive and significant (β = 0.18, p < 0.01). Prior literature did not predict this finding. We verify the result holds across OLS, IV, and diff-in-diff specifications.

**What changed:**
- ~~It is shown in Table 3~~ → **Table 3 shows** (the table acts)
- ~~was not expected based on~~ → **did not predict** (active verb, clearer agent)
- ~~was verified through~~ → **We verify... holds across** (you're the agent)

# Rule 20: Keep Related Words Together

**The position of words in a sentence is the principal means of showing their relationship.**

---

## The Principle

Strunk & White's famous example:

❌ **Ambiguous:** New York's first commercial human-sperm bank opened Friday with semen samples from eighteen men frozen in a stainless steel tank.

*(Were the men frozen?)*

✓ **Clear:** New York's first commercial human-sperm bank opened Friday when semen samples were taken from eighteen men. The samples were then frozen and stored in a stainless steel tank.

---

## Sentence-Level Examples

**From Economics/Social Science Writing:**

❌ We only tested three models
✓ We tested only three models *(tested 3, not 4)*

❌ Firms with high leverage that defaulted were likely to fire CEOs
✓ High-leverage firms that defaulted were likely to fire CEOs

❌ Using propensity score matching, the effect was estimated
✓ Using propensity score matching, we estimate the effect

❌ The results that were obtained show significance
✓ The results show significance

---

## More Sentence Examples

❌ Students performed better who received tutoring
✓ Students who received tutoring performed better

❌ The manager should be hired who has five years experience
✓ The manager who has five years experience should be hired

❌ We examine overconfidence in this paper
✓ In this paper, we examine overconfidence

❌ A correlation exists between X and Y that is significant
✓ A significant correlation exists between X and Y

---

## Academic Writing: Modifier Placement

❌ **Ambiguous**
> We only tested three models.

**Meaning 1:** We tested three models (not four).
**Meaning 2:** We merely tested models (didn't implement them).

✓ **Clear**
> We tested only three models. *(limits the number)*
> We only tested the models (we didn't implement them). *(limits the action)*

**Rule:** Place "only" immediately before the word it modifies.

---

## Long Noun Phrases

Academic writing creates ambiguity with long modifying phrases:

❌ **Ambiguous**
> Firms with high leverage that defaulted during the crisis were more likely to fire their CEOs.

**Which firms?**
- High-leverage firms that defaulted?
- High-leverage firms, of which some defaulted?

---

## Long Noun Phrases

✓ **Clear Option 1:** Firms that defaulted during the crisis were more likely to fire their CEOs if they had high leverage.

✓ **Clear Option 2:** High-leverage firms that defaulted during the crisis were more likely to fire their CEOs.

✓ **Clearest:** Among firms that defaulted during the crisis, those with high leverage were 2.3 times more likely to fire their CEOs.

**Bonus:** Last version adds a magnitude.

---

## Subject-Verb Separation

❌ **Awkward (subject and verb separated)**
> CEO overconfidence, despite being extensively studied in the corporate finance literature and examined from multiple theoretical perspectives, remains an important topic.

**Problem:** 17 words between subject (overconfidence) and verb (remains).

✓ **Clear**
> CEO overconfidence remains an important topic despite extensive study from multiple theoretical perspectives.

---

## Dangling Modifiers (Participial Phrases)

❌ **Dangling**
> Using propensity score matching, the treatment effect was estimated.

**Problem:** "Using propensity score matching" modifies "treatment effect." The effect didn't use matching; YOU did.

✓ **Correct**
> Using propensity score matching, we estimate the treatment effect.

✓ **Alternative**
> We estimate the treatment effect using propensity score matching.

---

## Exercise: Fix the Modifier Placement

What's wrong? Fix it.

> A manager of proven ability should be given this position who has worked in the industry for at least five years.

**Your turn:** Where should the relative clause go?

---

## Exercise: Answer

❌ Original: who has worked in the industry modifies "position" (wrong!)

✓ **Fixed:** A manager who has worked in the industry for at least five years and has proven ability should be given this position.

✓ **Better:** This position requires a manager with five years of industry experience and proven ability.

✓ **Best:** We seek a manager with 5+ years industry experience for this position.

**Progression:** Grammatical → Clear → Concise

---

# Rule 19: Express Coordinate Ideas in Similar Form

**Parallel construction requires that expressions similar in content and function be outwardly similar.**

---

## The Principle

Strunk & White's example:

❌ **Not Parallel:** Formerly, science was taught by the textbook method, while now the laboratory method is employed.

✓ **Parallel:** Formerly, science was taught by the textbook method; now it is taught by the laboratory method.

**Pattern:** Formerly X; now Y. Both clauses have the same structure.

---

## Sentence-Level Examples

**From Economics/Social Science Writing:**

❌ This paper identifies the effect, documents the mechanism, and policy implications are discussed
✓ This paper identifies the effect, documents the mechanism, and discusses policy implications

❌ The study is rigorous, innovative, and has practical relevance
✓ The study is rigorous, innovative, and practically relevant

❌ We collected data, the model was estimated, and then tested robustness
✓ We collected data, estimated the model, and tested robustness

---

## More Sentence Examples

❌ **H1:** Diversity increases innovation. **H2:** Performance is higher when boards are diverse
✓ **H1:** Diversity increases innovation. **H2:** Diversity increases performance

❌ The ceremony was both long and very tedious
✓ The ceremony was both long and tedious

❌ Either you must grant the request or his ill will will be incurred
✓ You must either grant the request or incur his ill will

❌ Firms can improve quality, reduce costs, or increasing efficiency
✓ Firms can improve quality, reduce costs, or increase efficiency

---

## Academic Applications: Lists

Parallel structure is essential for lists:

❌ **Not Parallel**
> This paper makes three contributions: first, identifying the causal effect of X on Y; second, the role of Z is documented; and third, we show implications for policy.

**Problem:** Three different structures (gerund, passive noun phrase, active clause).

---

## Academic Applications: Lists

✓ **Parallel (Gerunds)**
> This paper makes three contributions. First, identifying the causal effect of X on Y. Second, documenting the role of Z as a mechanism. Third, showing implications for policy design.

✓ **Parallel (Active Clauses)**
> This paper makes three contributions. First, we identify the causal effect of X on Y. Second, we document the role of Z. Third, we show implications for policy.

**Either works; just be consistent.**

---

## Hypothesis Statements

❌ **Not Parallel**
> **H1:** Firms with female directors adopt more family-friendly policies.
> **H2:** Innovation is higher when boards are diverse.
> **H3:** The presence of institutional investors moderates this relationship.

**Problem:** H1 starts with subject, H2 starts with outcome, H3 starts with different construction.

---

## Hypothesis Statements

✓ **Parallel**
> **H1:** Board gender diversity increases adoption of family-friendly policies.
> **H2:** Board gender diversity increases firm innovation.
> **H3:** Institutional ownership strengthens both effects.

**Pattern:** All three start with subject → verb → outcome.

**Bonus:** Reader can now see the logical connections between hypotheses.

---

## Paper Contributions

❌ **Not Parallel**
> We contribute to the literature in three ways. First, we provide causal evidence using a natural experiment. Second, documentation of a new mechanism. Third, our findings have policy implications.

✓ **Parallel**
> We contribute to the literature in three ways. First, we provide causal evidence using a natural experiment. Second, we document a new mechanism (Z). Third, we show actionable policy implications.

**Pattern:** First, we X. Second, we Y. Third, we Z.**

---

## Correlative Expressions

Use parallel structure with correlatives (both/and, either/or, not only/but also):

❌ **Not Parallel:** It was both a long ceremony and very tedious.
✓ **Parallel:** The ceremony was both long and tedious.

❌ **Not Parallel:** Either you must grant his request or incur his ill will.
✓ **Parallel:** You must either grant his request or incur his ill will.

**Rule:** What follows "both" must match what follows "and."

---

## Exercise: Make It Parallel

Fix the parallelism:

> Our study has three strengths. First, large sample size. Second, we use a randomized design. Third, the results have been replicated.

**Your turn:** Make all three match.

---

## Exercise: Answer

✓ **Parallel (Noun Phrases):**
> Our study has three strengths: a large sample (n = 2,847), a randomized design, and successful replication.

✓ **Parallel (Active Clauses):**
> Our study has three strengths. First, we use a large sample (n = 2,847). Second, we employ a randomized design. Third, we successfully replicate prior findings.

**Either works; mixing them does not.**

## Strategic Positioning for Maximum Effect

**In this section, you will master:**

- **Rule 22:** Place the emphatic words of a sentence at the end

---

# Rule 22: Place the Emphatic Words of a Sentence at the End

**The proper place for the word or group of words that you want to make most prominent is usually the end.**

---

## The Principle

> The effectiveness of the periodic sentence arises from the prominence it gives to the main statement.

—Strunk & White

**Why?** The end of a sentence/paragraph/section is where the reader's attention peaks. Don't waste it on weak words.

---

## Sentence-Level Examples

**From Economics/Social Science Writing:**

❌ CEO overconfidence matters for corporate decisions, according to our findings
✓ Our findings show CEO overconfidence matters for corporate decisions

❌ This steel is used for razors, because of its hardness
✓ Because of its hardness, this steel is used for razors

❌ Overconfidence affects decisions across many domains
✓ Decisions across many domains are distorted by overconfidence

---

## More Sentence Examples

❌ We examine whether feedback reduces overconfidence in this study
✓ In this study, we examine whether feedback reduces overconfidence

❌ The intervention was successful, as shown by the data
✓ The data show the intervention was successful

❌ Random assignment rules out selection bias, which is important
✓ Random assignment rules out selection bias entirely

❌ The policy increased employment significantly, we find
✓ We find the policy increased employment significantly

---

## Word Level: Sentence Endings

❌ **Weak Ending**
> Overconfidence affects economic decisions across many domains.

✓ **Strong Ending**
> Economic decisions across many domains are distorted by overconfidence.

**What changed:** "Overconfidence" (the key concept) now ends the sentence, receiving emphasis.

---

## Sentence Level Examples

❌ **Weak:** This steel is principally used for making razors, because of its hardness.

✓ **Strong:** Because of its hardness, this steel is principally used for making razors.

**Academic version:**

❌ **Weak:** CEO overconfidence matters for corporate decisions, according to our findings.

✓ **Strong:** Our findings show CEO overconfidence matters for corporate decisions.

---

## Paragraph Level: Introduction Paragraphs

❌ **Weak Ending (What Students Write)**
> Overconfidence affects economic decisions in hiring (Kausel et al., 2016), trading (Barber & Odean, 2001), and entrepreneurship (Camerer & Lovallo, 1999), among others. We examine whether personalized feedback can reduce overconfidence in this study.

**Problem:** Paragraph ends with "in this study" - filler words, no impact.

---

## Paragraph Level: Introduction Paragraphs

✓ **Strong Ending (Revised)**
> Overconfidence distorts economic decisions in hiring (Kausel et al., 2016), trading (Barber & Odean, 2001), and entrepreneurship (Camerer & Lovallo, 1999). Existing interventions have had limited success. We test whether personalized feedback—showing individuals exactly where they rank—can reduce overconfidence.

**What changed:**
- Ends with your key insight: "exactly where they rank" (the mechanism)
- Deleted "in this study" (pointless filler)
- Reader remembers your contribution

---

## Section Level: How to End Your Introduction

Every introduction paragraph should end with its punch line:

**Paragraph 1** (Gap): ...But **no study has tested whether personalized feedback reduces overconfidence.**

**Paragraph 2** (Your approach): ...We use a field experiment with 311 subjects to **provide causal evidence.**

**Paragraph 3** (Your finding): ...Personalized feedback reduces overconfidence by **18 percentage points (p < 0.001).**

**Pattern:** Each paragraph builds to its strongest claim.

---

## Conclusion Endings: Weak vs. Strong

❌ **Weak (How Students End Papers)**
> These findings contribute to the literature on behavioral economics and suggest several avenues for future research. We hope this work will inspire others to investigate related questions. There are many directions for further study.

**What the reader remembers:** "There are many directions..." (vague, pointless)

---

## Conclusion Endings: Weak vs. Strong

✓ **Strong (How to End Papers)**
> These findings suggest a practical intervention: organizations can reduce costly overconfidence simply by showing employees their actual performance relative to peers—no elaborate training needed, just honest feedback.

**What the reader remembers:** "just honest feedback" (your actionable insight)

**Bonus:** This demonstrates Rule 22 by placing "just honest feedback" at the very end.

---

## Abstract Endings

The last sentence of your abstract is prime real estate:

❌ **Weak:** Our findings contribute to the literature and have important implications.

✓ **Strong:** This overinvestment costs shareholders $8.7 million annually per firm.

✓ **Strong:** Our findings explain 34% of cross-firm variation in investment rates.

✓ **Strong:** Regulators could use our screening tool to identify at-risk firms before crises occur.

**Pattern:** End with your most concrete, actionable, or surprising finding.

---

## Results Section: Building to the Key Finding

Structure your results to climax:

**Table 1:** Baseline results → End with: "The treatment effect is positive and significant."

**Table 2:** Heterogeneity → End with: "The effect is twice as large for women."

**Table 3:** Mechanism → End with: "We find clear evidence that confidence updating drives the result."

**Robustness paragraph:** End with: "The finding is robust across all 15 specifications."

**Pattern:** Each unit ends with its strongest claim.

---

## The Emphatic Order of a Paper

Place your best material where it has most impact:

**Abstract:** End with most surprising/actionable finding
**Introduction:** Each paragraph ends with its key point
**Results:** Build from baseline → heterogeneity → mechanism (climax)
**Conclusion:** Last paragraph ends with "so what?" (policy/theory implication)

**This entire lecture demonstrates Rule 22 by placing it last!**

---

## Exercise: Reorder for Impact

Which ending is stronger?

**Version A:** We test this hypothesis using a field experiment with random assignment, which allows us to rule out selection bias.

**Version B:** Random assignment in our field experiment rules out selection bias.

**Your turn:** Which puts the key point at the end?

---

## Exercise: Answer

**Version B is stronger:** "selection bias" is the key point (what you're solving), so it goes at the end.

**Even Better:** Our field experiment uses random assignment, eliminating selection bias entirely.

**Progression:**
- Weak: "which allows us to rule out selection bias" (buried in subordinate clause)
- Better: "rules out selection bias" (main clause, end position)
- Best: "eliminating selection bias entirely" (end position + emphatic adverb)

## Your Toolkit for Clear, Vigorous Writing

**Today we mastered 7 of the 43 rules—the ones that matter most for academic writing:**

**Foundation:**
1. **Rule 17:** Omit needless words - every word must earn its place
2. **Rule 16:** Use definite, specific, concrete language - give magnitudes, not adjectives

**Structure:**
4. **Rule 14:** Use the active voice - make your agent clear

**Impact:**
7. **Rule 22:** Place emphatic words at the end - build to your punch line

## The Philosophy

> Vigorous writing is concise. A sentence should contain no unnecessary words, a paragraph no unnecessary sentences, for the same reason that a drawing should have no unnecessary lines and a machine no unnecessary parts.

> This requires not that the writer make all sentences short or avoid all detail, but that **every word tell**.

—William Strunk Jr., *The Elements of Style*
